NOLI ME TANGERE

______________________________________________________


Major Characters

Crisostomo Ibarra: A young man who returns from Europe and wants to build a school. Symbolizes patriotic youth who believe in education and peaceful reform.

María Clara: Ibarra's sweetheart; actually the daughter of Padre Damaso. Symbolizes the gentle, oppressed Filipina, and the Motherland herself.

Elias: A boatman and protector of Ibarra who wishes for the nation to change. Symbolizes the citizen who longs for change and is willing to sacrifice.



Friars and Those in Power

Padre Damaso: An arrogant and abusive Franciscan priest. Symbolizes the abuse of the friars and of the church.

Padre Salvi: A quiet but hypocritical and lecherous curate. Symbolizes the hypocrisy and evil hidden behind religion.

Padre Sibyla: A shrewd Dominican priest. Symbolizes the watchful, calculating power of the church.

Alferez: The head of the Guardia Civil. Symbolizes the abuse of military and authority.

Doña Consolacion: The cruel and oppressive wife of the Alferez. Symbolizes a person who abuses power once she gains it.



Filipinos Who Cling to Power

Kapitan Tiago: The recognized father of María Clara; wealthy and pro-Spanish. Symbolizes Filipinos who bow to foreigners for status and wealth.

Doña Victorina: A pretentious Filipina who pretends to be Spanish. Symbolizes colonial mentality.

Don Tiburcio de Espadaña: A fake doctor and the husband of Doña Victorina. Symbolizes fraud and lack of real ability.

Alfonso Linares: María Clara's suitor, introduced by Padre Damaso. Symbolizes opportunism.



The Oppressed and the Good

Sisa: A mother who loses her sanity after her sons go missing. Symbolizes the suffering mother and the oppressed nation.

Basilio and Crispin: Brothers and sons of Sisa; sacristans who are falsely accused and tortured. Symbolizes child victims of abuse and injustice.

Pilosopo Tasyo: A wise old man whom others call mad. Symbolizes brilliant ideas that society is not yet ready to understand.

Don Rafael Ibarra: Ibarra's father, a good man who died in prison. Symbolizes a victim of injustice.

Don Filipo: A liberal lieutenant mayor. Symbolizes a just and courageous leader.

Tiya Isabel: María Clara's aunt. Symbolizes the traditional and religious outlook.



EL FILIBUSTERISMO

______________________________________________________


Major Characters

Simoun (Crisostomo Ibarra): A wealthy jeweler and mysterious adviser to the Captain General. He is secretly Ibarra from Noli Me Tangere, returned to take revenge and plan a bloody revolution by blowing up a wedding feast. He hides behind blue-tinted glasses and a beard. Symbolizes vengeance and violent means of change.

Basilio: Sisa's surviving son and a medical student about to graduate. At first loyal to Simoun, he chooses to put his studies and his love for Juli first. Symbolizes youth drawn to peaceful progress.

Isagani: An idealistic poet and student, nephew of Padre Florentino, and sweetheart of Paulita Gomez. Symbolizes patriotic, passionate youth who believe in reform and oppose violent uprising.

Padre Florentino: A retired priest and Isagani's uncle. Wise and morally upright, he receives Simoun in his final moments and voices the novel's message: freedom is won through endurance and virtue.

Kabesang Tales (Telesforo Juan de Dios): A hardworking farmer from San Diego whose land is seized by the friars. He later becomes the outlaw Matanglawin and joins Simoun's cause. Symbolizes the oppressed people forced to fight back.

Juli (Julia): Kabesang Tales's daughter and Basilio's sweetheart. To free her father, she becomes a servant of Hermana Penchang, and later jumps from a convent window to escape Padre Camorra. Symbolizes a young woman sacrificed by poverty.



Students and Friends

Paulita Gomez: Isagani's beautiful sweetheart who later leaves him and marries Juanito Pelaez for wealth and security. Symbolizes choosing comfort over love.

Juanito Pelaez: A spoiled, deceitful classmate and favorite of Padre Camorra. He marries Paulita and is known for flattery and cowardice. Symbolizes opportunism and showiness.

Placido Penitente: A bright student who loses his drive to study after an unjust dispute with a professor. He is later killed in Simoun's plot. Symbolizes youth whose dreams are broken by a rotten system.

Makaraig: A wealthy student leader who headed the plan to establish the Spanish-language academy. Symbolizes youth leadership for reform.



The Friars

Padre Camorra: A lecherous, corrupt friar of Tiani who pursues Juli and uses his power against her. Symbolizes the abuse and wickedness of the friars.

Padre Salvi: The wicked former curate of San Diego, now Provincial of the Franciscans. Symbolizes hypocrisy and manipulation.

Padre Sibyla: A Dominican vice-rector and rival of the Franciscans. Intelligent, calculating, and opposed to Filipino reform. Symbolizes the church's calculated power.



Government and Colonial Society

Captain General: The powerful governor-general who is influenced and manipulated by Simoun. He seeks amusement and comfort. Symbolizes a negligent government.

Don Custodio (Jose Buenaventura): An arrogant, lazy "liberal" councilor who decides on the Spanish-language academy selfishly and carelessly. Symbolizes government neglect and indifference.

IMPACT

______________________________________


Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo have a big impact on us as Filipinos because they help us understand what our ancestors experienced during the Spanish period. These novels show the unfair treatment, corruption, abuse of power, and struggles that Filipinos faced in the past. Through the experiences of the characters, we can see how difficult life was for many Filipinos during that time.

Through these stories, we learn the importance of freedom, education, equality, and standing up for what is right. They also remind us that we should not allow others to be treated unfairly and that everyone deserves respect and equal rights. We also realize how important it is to speak up against wrong actions and fight for a better future. These novels help us appreciate the sacrifices of our heroes, especially Dr. Jose Rizal, who used his writings to show the problems in our country and inspire people to fight for change. Instead of using violence, Rizal used his knowledge and words to open the eyes of Filipinos and make them aware of the situation in society.

Even today, the lessons from these novels are still important because some of the problems shown in the stories can still be seen in our society. They encourage us to become more aware of what is happening around us and to learn from the mistakes of the past.

These novels remind us that our history has many lessons that we should never forget. They help us understand the importance of freedom, respect, and equality. As Filipinos, we should carry these lessons in our daily lives, appreciate the sacrifices of our heroes, and do our part in making our country a better place for everyone.

  • AXL ANDREW M. ALON


    • Watching and understanding Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo helped me realize the hard work and sacrifices of the people who came before us. Both films reminded me that Philippine history is not just a list of dates. It is the story of people who lived, suffered, and hoped for change. My favorite character is Elias. Because of his sacrifices, his story continues to inspire us to rise up and fight for change. The inequality, corruption, and abuse of power in the story are still happening today. As some people say, history repeats itself. In the end, we can only count on each other, because we cannot always depend on the government, especially in our country, which is full of corrupt politicians and political dynasties.

    Simoun, who was once Ibarra, gave up on peaceful reform and chose revenge instead. His story made me think about how injustice can change a person. I understood his anger, but I also saw that violence cannot truly heal a society. Father Florentino’s words reminded me that real change must come with responsibility, just like great power comes with great responsibility. His words also showed that suffering must have meaning, because everything happens for a reason. Basilio and Isagani showed me that the youth carry the hope of the nation for the next generation.

    In conclusion, both films taught me that change begins with awareness and courage. Rizal wants Filipinos today to love their country and to depend on themselves, because the government alone is not the answer to growth. I realized that education, honesty, and love for country are not small things. They are ways of continuing the work Rizal started.

  • ARRIAN MAY V. CASISON


    • Rizal’s two novels show that a nation’s problems come from a system that rewards silence. In the Noli, Ibarra believes in peaceful reform, but abusive friars, weak officials, and indifferent elites crush his hopes, and Sisa’s tragedy shows who pays the price. In the El Fili, Simoun chooses revenge and corruption, yet his plan fails, suggesting that change built on hatred cannot bring real freedom.

    What stays with me is how relevant both books still are. Corruption, inequality, and silence in the face of injustice did not end with the Spanish era. Rizal’s message is that loving the country means taking responsibility, and acting with courage and integrity.

  • GOEF AUSTERO


    • Honestly when we first got assigned Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo I thought it was just going to be two super long books I had to survive. But the more I read the more it hit me that these aren't just old school stories. They're about people getting hurt by a system that was supposed to protect them and some of it still feels way too real today.

    In the Noli Ibarra comes home from Europe full of hope. He believes that if he builds a school and does things the right way things will get better. I kind of admired that and also kind of wanted to tell him bro it's not that simple. The friars and the officials and everyone with power just shut him down. Then there's Sisa. Her part honestly broke me. She lost her sons and then lost her mind and nobody really cared. That's when I realized Rizal wasn't only writing about politics. He was writing about real people who suffer when no one speaks up.

    Then El Fili comes in and the mood is totally different. It's darker and angrier. Ibarra comes back as Simoun the rich jeweler and he's not the hopeful guy anymore. He's bitter and wants revenge. At first I thought maybe he's right since peaceful ways didn't work. But his plan falls apart because it came from pain and anger and not from really wanting to help the people. Kabesang Tales also stuck with me. He lost his land and his family and everything he worked for and he ended up becoming someone he never wanted to be. It showed me how a broken system can really change a person.

    The part that stayed with me most was Padre Florentino. He's calm and he doesn't shout but he says that change needs a just cause and the right means and that sacrifice is what makes a nation worth saving. I think Rizal is telling us that revenge isn't the answer and that being silent or just not caring is part of the problem too.

    Reading both novels made me look at my own life a little differently. We're not under Spain anymore but there's still corruption and still people who get ignored and still a lot of us who say wala naman akong magagawa. Rizal makes me think that we can do something even if it's small. Study hard and speak up when something is wrong and care about other people. That's what loving your country means to me now. It's not just something you memorize for a test. It's something you actually have to live out.
