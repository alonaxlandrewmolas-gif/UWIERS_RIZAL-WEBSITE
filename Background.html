NOLI ME TANGERE

______________________________________________________

How it came to be: In 1884, Rizal was studying in Madrid when he conceived the idea. He was influenced by books about injustice and oppression, such as Uncle Tom’s Cabin, and by what he knew of the friars’ power in the Philippines.

A group project that failed: He first proposed that fellow Filipinos in Madrid write the novel together. The plan fell apart, so he wrote it alone.

Written in pieces across Europe: He began it in Madrid, continued in Paris, and wrote the final chapters in Germany (Wilhelmsfeld and Berlin). He finished it in Berlin in February 1887.

Money trouble: Rizal ran out of funds and could not pay for printing. His friend Maximo Viola lent him the money, and the first copies came off a Berlin press in March 1887.

EL FILIBUSTERISMO

______________________________________________________

    Jose Rizal’s second novel and the sequel to Noli Me Tangere. It is darker, angrier and more political.

    Started in 1887 in Calamba, shortly after he returned from Europe. He revised and continued it in London (1888), then in Paris and Brussels.

    Finished in 1891 (March, in Biarritz, France). Rizal was disappointed with the results and the cost, and he had little money for printing.

    Dedicated to Gomez, Burgos and Zamora (GOMBURZA), the three priests executed in 1872. Rizal said their deaths inspired him to fight for justice.

    Main themes: Greed, Corruption, and Justice, the failure of violent revolution that comes without good motives, sacrifice, and the need for educated and moral leaders.

MI ULTIMO ADIOS

______________________________________________________

“Mi último adiós” is the farewell poem written by José Rizal, the Philippine national hero, the night before his execution on December 30, 1896. Rizal wrote it in Spanish while imprisoned in Fort Santiago in Manila, after being convicted of rebellion, sedition, and conspiracy by a military court. The Spanish colonial government held him responsible for inspiring the Philippine Revolution, even though he had not supported the armed uprising led by the Katipunan. Rizal was shot at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park, Luneta) on December 30, 1896, at the age of 35. Today the poem is considered one of the great works of Philippine literature, and it is commonly studied in Philippine schools, including in courses on Rizal’s life and writings.

IMPORTANT FACTS

______________________________________________________
  • 1872: The three martyr priests GomBurZa (Gómez, Burgos, and Zamora) were executed.
  • 1884 to 1887: He began Noli Me Tangere in Madrid in 1884, continued it in Paris, and finished it in Berlin, Germany, in 1887.
  • 1891: El Filibusterismo, translated as The Reign of Greed or The Filibuster, was published. It continues the story of Noli Me Tangere, with Ibarra returning as Simoun and Basilio now a medical student. It is dedicated to Gomburza.
  • 1892: He founded La Liga Filipina, a group that pushed for peaceful reform.
  • 1896: At his trial, the Spanish authorities cited the novels as evidence against him. By then, they had already strengthened nationalist feeling.
